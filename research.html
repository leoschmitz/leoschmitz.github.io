Real-time, high-quality isosurfaces on the GPU

My master's research at UFRGS made high-quality isosurface extraction run at interactive rates on commodity GPUs. It received a SIBGRAPI best paper invitation and was published in Computer Graphics Forum.

The problem

CT and MRI scanners and physics simulations produce huge 3D datasets. To work with them, you often extract an isosurface: a triangle mesh of everything at one density value, such as bone, tissue or a material boundary.

The standard algorithm, Marching Cubes, is fast but makes badly shaped triangles and rounds off sharp edges. Methods that fix this were too slow for interactive use on large volumes. My goal was to get both: good mesh quality and real-time speed.

What I built

  • GPU Macet. A parallel version of Macet, the Marching Cubes extension that moves grid edges to avoid degenerate, sliver-shaped triangles.
  • Table-driven Dual Contouring. A new formulation where a lookup table defines quad topology. It is simple, cache-friendly and fits GPU stream expansion with either geometry shaders or Histogram Pyramids.
  • Particle-based feature approximation. Vertex placement that keeps sharp edges and corners using trilinear interpolation, without the SVD or QR matrix decompositions classic Dual Contouring needs.
  • Hardware analysis. Profiling with industry GPU tools to find the bottlenecks of isosurface extraction and to evaluate options for future GPU designs.
